The different behaviors that can be given to a vehicle to steer itself. The behaviors are separated into Simple and Group. Use the radio to select which behaviors from that specific group.

Simple Vehicle

This is onto which the steering behaviors are applied. The vehicles in this simulation are denoted by a triangle with its vertex pointed to the direction of motion.

Vehicle has few important properties:

  • Position: Self explanatory
  • Velocity: This is modified by applying forces
  • Max Force: A typical force which adjusts the velocity
  • Max Speed: Self explanatory

For the physics of the simple vehicle model, at each simulation step, behaviorally determined steering forces are applied to the vehicle. That force is added to the old velocity to produce a new velocity, which is then truncated by max_speed. Finally, the velocity is added to the old position.

steering_force = truncate(steering_direction, max_force) velocity = truncate(velocity + steering_force, max_speed) position = position + velocity

Simple Steering Behaviors

Seek steers the vehicle towards a specified target. The desired velocity is a vector in the direction from the character to the target. The steering vector is the difference between this desired velocity and the character's current velocity.

desired_velocity = normalize(position - target) * max_speed steering_force = desired_velocity - velocity

Flee is simply the opposite of Seek and acts to steer the character so that its velocity is radially aligned away from the target.

Pursuit is similar to seek but for a moving target. It requires a prediction of the target's future position.

The prediction should be made in a way that it should be further ahead when the target is far and closer to the target when the pursuer is near.

predicted_target = target + prediction steering_force = seek(predicted_target)

The idea is to steer through a cluttered environment by dodging obstacles. Note that Flee will always cause a character to steer away from a given location, whereas obstacle avoidance takes action only when a nearby obstacle lies directly in front of the character. This behavior will use the nearest obstacle to calculate the avoidance force to be applied to the vehicle.

The first step is to look ahead to find the nearest obstacle. This is achieved by calculating a vector in the same direction as the vehicle some units in front.

Next we check for collisions with all the objects in the environment. Considering the objects as circles, the line intersects circle algorithm is used to check for collision.

What is line intersects circle algorithm? Simply check for the distance between the ahead vector's end and object's center. If it is less or equal to radius of the object ... Collision!

Lastly we calculate the force to be applied.

Dot Product:

Given 2 vectors U(ab) and V(ac), the dot product of them is given by the following formula:

U ⋅ V = |U| × |V| × cos(θ), where θ is the angle between the vectors.

Another formula to represent Dot Product is:

U ⋅ V = Ux × Vx + Uy × Vy

Scalar Projection:

Given 2 vectors U(ab) and V(ac), if a light source casts a shadow of U on V (ad), then that is vector projection (VP) and it's magnitude is scalar projection (|VP| = SP). Here ∠adb forms a right angle and db forms a normal to vector V.

If θ is ∠bad, then cos(θ) = SP / |U|
i.e. SP = |U| × cos(θ)

Revisiting the Dot Product equation...

U ⋅ V = |U| × |V| × cos(θ)
⇒ U ⋅ V = |U| × cos(θ), when |V| is 1
⇒ SP = U ⋅ normalize(V)

As the name suggests, the vehicle follows the nearest wall at a distance.

As a first step, the future position of the vehicle is predicted. Next the nearest wall is found from it. Then calculate a suitable point so that it is a paticular distance (offset) from the found wall, and seek it.

But how do we find the suitable point?
Drop a normal from the predicted position to the wall, then on the normal find a point that is a offset from the wall. This is where we use Scalar Projection.

As the name suggests, the vehicle follows a path and stays on it.

As a first step, the future position of the vehicle is predicted. Next it is checked if the predicted position is on the path. Because if it is on the path, the vehicle is on the right course and nothing needs to be done.

But how do we do check if the predicted position is on the path?
Drop a normal from the predicted position to the path, then get the distance between them. This is where Scalar Projection comes to play.

If the predicted position is not on the path, then simply seek the normal point.

In case the path has multiple segments, find the nearest segment and the algorithm continues.

Complex Steering Behaviors

There are three basic group behaviors which act on a, well, group of vehicles. the resultant force that acts upon the vehicle is dependent on state of surrounding vehicles.

Separation:

Separation gives the vehicle ability to keep distance from other vehicles. This is used to prevent crowding.

At it's core this checks for nearby vehicles and steers away. For every neighbor, calculate force pointing away from it, weight by distance (closer the vehicle, larger the force), then average the force to be applied.

Alignment:

Alignment gives the vehicle ability to align itself according to other vehicles. This is calculated by taking the average velocity of every nearby vehicles.

Cohesion:

Cohesion gives the vehicle ability to approach somewhere as a group with other vehicles. This is by figuring out the average location of nearby vehicles and then steer towards it.

This is where we start combining behaviors to make stuff more interesting. Here, a bunch of vehicles are initiated to follow the Path.

To achieve this, along with the Path Following behavior, Separation is also added so that the vehicles don't group together.
