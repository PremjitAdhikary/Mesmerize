It all started with a puzzle.

The owner of a banana plantation has a camel. He wants to transport his 300 bananas to the market. The distance between his banana plantation and the market is about 100 kilometers. So he decided to take his camel to carry the bananas. The camel can carry a maximum of 100 bananas at a time, and it eats one banana for every kilometer it travels.

At first, it seems impossible, but of course, there is a trick to it.

This is an optimisation problem and to better explain it to my daughter, I worked on this page. I had explained to her on paper by reducing the distance to 10 km. For 100 km, it made sense to code it.

In the viewport is a representation of a path in yellow winding right and left alternatively from top to bottom. At every km there is a milestone, starting from owner's banana plantation at 0 km till the market at 10 (or 100 based on Distance to Market selected) km. Any of the milestones with bananas, can be the plantation, the market, or drop points, will have a wooden box with a number on it denoting how many bananas are present at that milestone. The journey taken by to transport the bananas by the camel depicted as a creamish circle, will also display a number to denote the bananas it carries. Note: This keeps depleting as the camel travels.

Algorithms

The algorithms to solve the optimisation problem. The basic idea is that there have to be multiple trips to take all the bananas, and keep in mind that trip back also would require the camel to be fed.

This is the first solution (a bit of brute force-y) that came to mind.

Keep the camel moving back and forth between two milestones to move the bananas forward 1 km at a time. At one point in time, there will not be any bananas left in the previous milestone to go back to.

That's when we pick up everything and don't look back! There is a possibility that banana(s) are left behind before the last rush. This one, found on Google. This is more algebraic and makes sense once the underlying logic is understood. Also can be extrapolated to solve for different distances (say 1000 km).

The idea? For a 100 km route, with 300 bananas at the start, and a maximum carry capacity of 100 bananas, to maximise efficiency, the intermediate drop points should have multiples (or nearest multiple) of max capacity. With this in mind, the first intermediate drop point will have 200 bananas (100 bananas consumed for the back and forth journey). The second drop point will have 100 bananas (100 again consumed).

To move 300 bananas from the start to first intermediate drop point, the camel needs 5 trips (3 forward and 2 return). Let's say the first intermediate point is x km away. We have the following equation:

      300 − 5x = 200
      => x = 20

To move 200 bananas from the first intermediate drop point to the second, the camel needs 3 trips. Let the second intermediate point be y km from the first intermediate point. The equation is:

      200 − 3y = 100
      => y = 33.33 (for the puzzle, rounded down, which causes a banana to be left behind)

With the intermediate points determined, the rest becomes easy. Again, there is a possibility that banana(s) are left behind before the last rush.
